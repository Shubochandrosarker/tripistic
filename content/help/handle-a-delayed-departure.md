## Record a delay

1. Open the departure in the **operations centre**.
2. Set status to **Delayed**.
3. Record the reason and expected duration.
4. Choose **Notify booked travellers**.

Everyone on that departure receives the notice in one action, with the reason and new timing.

**Wait until you know the real delay.** One accurate message at 15 minutes beats three revisions across an hour.

## Cancel a departure

1. Set the departure status to **Cancelled**.
2. Record the reason — weather, vehicle, staffing, minimum numbers.
3. Choose whether to notify.
4. Confirm.

Every booking on the departure is released. Refunds remain your decision under your own cancellation terms.

Consider offering a move to another date before cancelling outright — a moved booking keeps the revenue and usually the goodwill.

## Report an incident

For anything involving traveller safety, injury, property, or a serious service failure, record an incident rather than a note.

Capture what happened, when, who was involved, severity, actions taken, and the follow-up owner. The record attaches to the departure and the customers involved and is permanent.

This is the audit trail that matters for adventure, educational, and group travel, and increasingly for procurement.

## Status reference

| Status | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| `SCHEDULED` | Normal, upcoming |
| `BOARDING` | Travellers gathering, check-in underway |
| `DEPARTED` | Underway |
| `DELAYED` | Running late — record reason and duration |
| `COMPLETED` | Finished |
| `CANCELLED` | Not running — bookings released |

## After the day

Everything is recorded on the departure's event timeline with actor and timestamp: status changes, notifications sent, incidents, notes, and route changes. That is what you review when working out whether a bad day was bad luck or a pattern.
